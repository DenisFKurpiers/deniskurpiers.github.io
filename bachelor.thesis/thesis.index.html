Staying with the topic of Diffeomorphic Image Registration, I tried to lay out the mathematical framework. To be discussed: do I even understand the problem at hand?

For brevity, I refer to "Computational anatomy: an emerging discipline" as OG (making the assumption it's the genesis paper) and "Computing Large Deformation Metric Mappings via Geodesic Flows of Diffeomorphisms" as PS (again making the assumption that it has been paradigm shifting).

OG talks about the Brown/Washington model (is it still relevant? idk): we can model anatomy as a quadruple \((\Omega, \mathcal{H}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{P})\), where the background space \(\Omega\) is defined as the union \(\Omega = \bigcup_{\alpha} M_{\alpha}\), where \(M_{\alpha}\) are 0, 1, 2, 3-dimensional manifolds, and \(\alpha \in I\), where \(I\) is the set of indexes. \(\mathcal{H}\) is the set of diffeomorphic transformations on the background space, \(\mathcal{H}: \Omega \leftrightarrow \Omega\). \(\mathcal{I}\) is the space of medical imagery and \(\mathcal{P}\) the set of probability measures on \(\mathcal{H}\).

OG notes that if \(\mathcal{H}\) is constructed to be rich enough (later mentioning an awesome dimension of \(10^7\)), we can operate under the basic assumption that there is essentially only one deformable template (\(I_{temp}\)), i.e., one anatomical ensemble \((\Omega, \mathcal{H}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{P})\), which forms a homogeneous space, or what they call homogeneous anatomy.

Note: This simply means that there is a single, ideal anatomy, and all normal biological variations can be generated by transforming this one template.

The basic property of this homogeneous anatomy is that it can be completely generated from one of its elements, defined mathematically as \(\mathcal{I} = \mathcal{H}I_{temp}\), \(I_{temp} \in \mathcal{I}\). In the homogeneous anatomy setting, all elements \(I \in \mathcal{I}\) are essentially the same modulo a diffeomorphism in \(\mathcal{H}\).

More generally, as for the disease state, an area that has received less attention than the study of normal anatomy, we shall require a family of templates \(\bigcup_{\alpha} I^{\alpha}_{temp}\) with perhaps varying dimensional transformation classes. The complete anatomy is built from collections of homogeneous spaces \(\mathcal{I}_{total} = \bigcup_{\alpha}(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}, \mathcal{H}^{\alpha})\).

Within this framework, we see that there are three principal components to computational anatomy:

  1. Computation of large deformation maps: Given any two elements $I_n, I_m \in \mathcal{I}$ in the same homogeneous anatomy $(\Omega, \mathcal{H}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{P})$, compute diffeomorphisms $h$ with inverses $h^{-1} \doteq \phi$ from one anatomy to the other $I_n \underset{\phi_{nm} \doteq h_{nm}^{-1}}{\overset{h_{nm}}{\rightleftharpoons}} I_m$. This is the principal method by which anatomical structures are understood, transferring the emphasis from the images $I \in \mathcal{I}$ to the structural transformations that generate them.
  2. Computation of empirical probability laws: Assume we are given populations of anatomical imagery and diffeomorphisms between them $I_n \underset{\phi_{nm} \doteq h_{nm}^{-1}}{\overset{h_{nm}}{\rightleftharpoons}} I_m$, $n,m = 1, \dots, N \subset \mathcal{H}$. The second component of computational anatomy calculates probability laws $P \in \mathcal{P}$ on $\mathcal{H}$ which represent anatomical variation reflected by the observed population.
  3. Computational inference on population and disease testing and classification: Given an anatomical representation in the form of prior probability measures on the deformable template representing the anatomy, perform Bayesian classification and testing for disease and anomaly.

Within the frameworks of the PS paper, I only care about number (1): Computation of large deformation maps I think.
